# What this course does not teach

An advisor's most valuable output is the cut list. Every item below is a real thing
engineers know, and every one of them is out.

- LeetCode hard, dynamic programming, graph algorithms
- Metaclasses, multiple inheritance, descriptors
- Design-pattern catalogues
- Django and Flask — FastAPI only, and only in week 11
- numpy, pandas, and the data-science stack
- Training or fine-tuning models; PyTorch; transformer maths
- Kubernetes, Terraform, cloud certifications
- Any frontend framework
- Threading and multiprocessing — `asyncio` only
- Databases and ORMs
- AutoGen, Swarm, and whichever framework launched this month
- Authentication beyond a shared API key
- CI/CD pipelines beyond one script

---

## Why

Every item here is learnable later in about a week, and **none of them is what stops a
career-changer from getting hired.**

The five gates this course aims at are: a Python fundamentals screen, a project
deep-dive, a verbal concepts round, a small practical build or debug, and junior-grade
system design. Nothing on the list above serves any of them.

Say this out loud in week 1. Students who know *why* something was cut stop worrying
that they are missing it, and that worry is a major cause of drift in month two.

---

## What to add afterwards, in order

If you finish and want more, roughly the order that pays back:

1. **A real embedding model** in place of week 9's TF-IDF — the natural next step, and
   the golden set already exists to judge it
2. **SQL and one database** — the most common gap in a junior AI engineer's toolkit
3. **pandas**, if the roles you are applying to mention data
4. **One cloud platform properly**, rather than three superficially
5. **Async in earnest** — week 7 only scratches it
